A quiet room for loud ideas

TARDIS is a suite of rooms in the Wold Center designed for acoustics research and professional recording. Its low noise floor and strong isolation make it a good home for work that needs controlled acoustic conditions: tracking a band, capturing a quiet solo instrument, measuring a sound source, or testing a prototype.

The studio serves as an interdisciplinary hub. Music and engineering faculty oversee it together, and it is open to faculty, faculty-supervised student projects, and students enrolled in Music Technology courses.

Using TARDIS

From idea to session

TARDIS is a shared resource, so time in the studio is planned and reserved.

  1. Share your project

    Fill out the project interest form or get in touch with Professor Chandler. Tell us what you want to make or measure.

  2. Get access

    Approved faculty projects, faculty-supervised student projects, and students in Music Technology courses can use the studio.

  3. Reserve your time

    Book sessions on the live room and control room calendars. Students can reserve up to four hours per week, and unreserved time is open to use.

  4. Record with support

    Music Technology work-study students help with setup and equipment. Sessions with more than two musicians are supervised by a work-study student.

Who can use it: Union faculty, faculty-supervised student projects, and students actively enrolled in Music Technology coursework.

Studio basics

  • Come with a plan and arrive on time.
  • Food stays in the entry space. Closed water bottles are fine.
  • Back up your session at the start and end, and keep files in your own folder on the TARDIS Data drive.
  • Equipment never leaves the studio, and control room gear stays connected.
  • Coil cables, return gear, and leave the room cleaner than you found it.
  • Keep playback at a safe level. The isolation is good; there's no need to test it.
